## Part 3 - Model Experiment

**Experiment: Bivariate Poisson correlation term**

The current model treats home and away goals as independent Poisson processes.
The bivariate Poisson adds a shared latent variable Z ~ Poisson(lam3):

  X = X* + Z, X* ~ Poisson(lambda_h - lam3)
  Y = Y* + Z, Y* ~ Poisson(lambda_a - lam3)

Key identity (derived during the experiment): P(home win), P(draw), P(away win)
under bivariate Poisson equal the independent Poisson probabilities computed
at shifted lambdas (lambda_h - lam3, lambda_a - lam3). So the experiment
reduces to: does globally shrinking both predicted lambdas improve Brier?

Design: tune lam3 on val1 (2024 matches), evaluate on val2 (2025 matches).

Grid-search results on val1 (1,231 matches):
- lam3=0.00: val1 Brier=0.17845
- lam3=0.10: val1 Brier=0.17813
- lam3=0.22: val1 Brier=0.17793 (best)
- lam3=0.30: val1 Brier=0.17800
- lam3=0.50: val1 Brier=0.17944

Best lam3 = 0.22 on val1. Applied to val2 (1,264 matches):
- Baseline (lam3=0) val2 Brier: 0.16570
- Bivariate (lam3=0.24) val2 Brier: 0.16524
- Delta: -0.00045

Verdict: **discarded**. The delta (-0.00045) is well within the standard error
for 1,264 matches (SE ~0.002). The trend on val1 is real but tiny. The lam3=0.24
value would mean subtracting 0.24 expected goals per team from every prediction,
a non-trivial shift with no structural justification in the feature space. The
improvement does not clear the bar for adding complexity to the pipeline.

Experiment script saved at `pipeline/model/experiment_bivariate_poisson.py`.
